BIC BENCHMARK PROFILE — Counter Culture Coffee (real company)

Compiled entirely from public sources (company website, trade press, B Lab, Wikipedia) via live web search on 2026-08-14. No internal, financial, or proprietary information about Counter Culture Coffee is included or implied. Every claim below carries a source URL and a confidence rating (high/med/low). Where a claim could not be verified from a primary or corroborated source, it is listed separately under "Assumed (unverified)." Nothing here should be read as a criticism or weakness of Counter Culture — the "delta questions" are prompts for an analyst to ask an up-and-coming roaster client, not judgments about Counter Culture itself.

Note on sourcing method: Counter Culture's own website (counterculturecoffee.com) was unreachable from this research environment (network egress restriction), so claims attributed to their transparency reports and site pages are drawn from search-engine result snippets and secondary coverage that quote or summarize those pages directly, not from a direct fetch of the source page. Confidence is capped at "med" for any claim relying solely on a snippet rather than a directly rendered primary page.

## 1. Customer Segments

- Wholesale café, restaurant, and hospitality accounts across the US (the company operates a dedicated "Training Centers Wholesale" program aimed at this segment). — source: counterculturecoffee.com/pages/training-centers-wholesale (via search index) — confidence: med
- Direct-to-consumer retail buyers, including via third-party retail channels (e.g., bagged whole-bean coffee sold on Amazon under the Counter Culture brand). — source: amazon.com product listing for Counter Culture Coffee whole bean — confidence: med
- Coffee professionals and industry attendees at Counter Culture's regional training centers (education-focused segment, distinct from direct coffee buyers). — source: counterculturecoffee.com/pages/training-centers (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** Which of your customer segments would you say is underserved the way a national training-center network implies deep investment in the "coffee professional" segment specifically — do you have anyone in your business who exists to serve baristas/roasters at other companies, not just your own buyers? How would you segment your wholesale accounts today if you had to name three tiers?

## 2. Value Propositions

- First company in the coffee industry to publish a transparency report disclosing FOB (Free on Board) prices paid to suppliers, starting in 2009. — source: sprudge.com, "The Transparent Trap: Counter Culture Releases Direct Trade Annual Report" / coffeehabitat.com — confidence: high
- Certified B Corporation, with a published B Impact Score that has been reported publicly (increase from 80.3 to 91.9 at 2023 recertification, per company blog). — source: bcorporation.net B Corp directory listing; counterculturecoffee.com blog "Increasing Our B Corp Score" (via search index) — confidence: med (certification status high, specific score figures med)
- Positions quality, sustainability, and education as the three pillars guiding the business, expressed annually through the transparency report format (sections on People, Planet, Products, Finances in recent years). — source: search summary of counterculturecoffee.com/pages/transparency-report-2025 (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** What is the one claim about your coffee you could publish externally, with numbers attached, the way Counter Culture publishes FOB prices — and what would it cost you to be able to make that claim honestly? Do you have anything like an annual public accountability document, even informally?

## 3. Channels

- Physical training center / retail locations reported in multiple cities including Durham NC (HQ), New York, Washington DC, Los Angeles, Seattle, Chicago, Boston, Philadelphia, Atlanta, Charleston SC, Asheville NC, Dallas, Miami, and Emeryville CA. — source: aggregated from counterculturecoffee.com/pages/training-centers, dailycoffeenews.com, sprudge.com, and Yelp business listings (via search index) — confidence: med (individual city list assembled from secondary listings, not verified against a single current primary source)
- E-commerce / direct online ordering, plus third-party retail distribution (e.g., Amazon). — source: counterculturecoffee.com; amazon.com listing — confidence: med
- Wholesale sales channel serving café and restaurant accounts directly, distinct from the training-center retail footprint. — source: counterculturecoffee.com/pages/training-centers-wholesale (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** How many of your channels are true multi-city physical presence versus a single roastery plus shipping — and does that gap matter for the customers you're actually trying to win, or is it a vanity comparison? What would a regional (not national) version of a training-center channel look like for you?

## 4. Customer Relationships

- Relationship model built around in-person education: training centers function as cupping labs and classroom space for wholesale and industry customers, not just point-of-sale retail. — source: sprudge.com coverage of Seattle training center; dailycoffeenews.com coverage of DC training center — confidence: med
- Long-tenure sourcing relationships are treated as a relationship metric and reported publicly (85% of green coffee purchased in 2022 came from partners of 5+ years, per Counter Culture's own 2022 transparency report). — source: counterculturecoffee.com/blogs/counter-culture-coffee/our-2022-transparency-report (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** What percentage of your own supplier relationships are 5+ years old, and could you produce that number if asked today? What's your equivalent of a "cupping lab" — a place where a customer experiences your quality decisions directly rather than just receiving the bag?

## 5. Revenue Streams

- Multiple revenue lines implied by public information: wholesale coffee sales to café/restaurant accounts, direct-to-consumer retail sales (online and third-party retail), and a paid or account-gated education/training function tied to the training centers. — source: counterculturecoffee.com site structure (via search index); amazon.com listing — confidence: low (stream existence is well-supported; no verified revenue split is available)
- No public revenue figures, revenue mix percentages, or company valuation could be verified — PitchBook and Tracxn list the company as privately held with no disclosed financials in the search results reviewed. — source: pitchbook.com company profile; tracxn.com company profile — confidence: high (as a statement that the figures are undisclosed)

**Delta questions:** If a benchmark company won't disclose its revenue mix publicly, what does that tell you about how much of your own revenue story you should be sharing with prospects versus keeping internal? What's your actual streams breakdown today, and which stream would you bet is under-monetized relative to the attention it gets?

## 6. Key Resources

- A dedicated headquarters and roasting facility in Durham, NC. — source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Culture_Coffee; tastingtable.com feature on Durham HQ — confidence: high
- A multi-decade sourcing relationship network, explicitly measured and reported (see Customer Relationships above). — source: counterculturecoffee.com 2022 transparency report (via search index) — confidence: med
- Brand equity built specifically around transparency and sourcing credibility, accumulated since the company's founding in 1995. — source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Culture_Coffee; specialtyfood.com leadership announcement — confidence: high

**Delta questions:** What's the single resource you couldn't replace or rebuild quickly if you lost it — and is it a person, a relationship, or a piece of equipment? Do you have anything that functions as accumulated "credibility capital" the way 17 years of published transparency reports does for Counter Culture, even at a much smaller scale?

## 7. Key Activities

- Publishing an annual transparency report every year since 2009 — a sustained, repeatable activity, not a one-time initiative. — source: coffeehabitat.com; sprudge.com — confidence: high
- Direct Trade sourcing and quality grading of green coffee, formalized as a certified policy starting in 2008. — source: sprudge.com "Counter Culture Releases Direct Trade Annual Report" — confidence: high
- Running ongoing in-person education/training programming across a multi-city training center network. — source: counterculturecoffee.com/pages/training-centers (via search index); multiple local press pieces on individual training center openings — confidence: med
- Roasting and forward-contracting a substantial share of annual green coffee purchases (99.16% forward-contracted at time of 2023 B Corp recertification, per company communication). — source: counterculturecoffee.com blog "Increasing Our B Corp Score" (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** Which of your key activities is genuinely repeatable and institutionalized versus dependent on one person's time and memory — and where does your own sample-to-quote or sourcing process fall on that spectrum? What would it take for your key activities to survive a founder's two-week vacation the way an annually-published report format implies a process, not a person?

## 8. Key Partners

- Green coffee growing/producer partners under Direct Trade relationships, with a meaningful share (85% in 2022, per company reporting) being 5+ year relationships. — source: counterculturecoffee.com 2022 transparency report (via search index) — confidence: med
- Trees, Water & People, an environmental nonprofit partner, credited with a joint clean-cookstove installation program in Honduras (393 cookstoves cited in a past report). — source: search summary of counterculturecoffee.com transparency report content (via search index) — confidence: med
- B Lab, as the certifying body for the company's B Corporation status. — source: bcorporation.net B Corp directory — confidence: high

**Delta questions:** Do you have a named nonprofit or third-party partner tied to a specific, countable outcome (like a cookstove count) that you could point to today — or is your sustainability story still qualitative? Which of your supplier relationships could you name publicly, with a number attached, the way Counter Culture names an 85% long-tenure figure?

## 9. Cost Structure

- Meaningful exposure to green coffee purchasing costs at scale — 2022 reporting cites 5,600,137 lbs of green coffee purchased that year, a volume that implies purchasing, quality-grading, and logistics cost structures well beyond a single-roastery operation. — source: counterculturecoffee.com 2022 transparency report (via search index) — confidence: med
- Multi-city real estate footprint (13+ training center / retail locations) implies a materially different fixed-cost base than a single-location roaster. — source: aggregated training center location coverage (see Channels section) — confidence: med
- Forward-contracting the large majority of green coffee purchases (99.16% cited at 2023 recertification) implies a cost structure that locks in pricing ahead of the market, trading flexibility for supply certainty. — source: counterculturecoffee.com blog "Increasing Our B Corp Score" (via search index) — confidence: med

**Delta questions:** What share of your own coffee purchases are forward-contracted versus bought reactively, and do you know that number off the top of your head? If you had a multi-city footprint's worth of fixed costs, what would have to be true about your revenue per location before that made sense — and how far are you from that today?
