# driftline.example

We roast specialty coffee in small batches out of one facility and sell it two ways — a direct-to-consumer subscription that ships every two or four weeks, and wholesale to independent cafés who put our coffee on their own menus. We started four years ago with me roasting on weekends and selling bags at the farmers market, and it grew into a real production operation faster than our process did. Right now we're at roughly $1.8M a year, split pretty evenly between the subscription side and wholesale, with a smaller slice from event and one-off retail sales. The subscription business runs fine — it's mostly automated through Shopify. Wholesale is where the strain is: we've got 26 café accounts and a waitlist of inbound leads we can't get through fast enough, because sampling and quoting still run through me personally instead of a documented process.

LEADERSHIP INTERVIEW — Driftline Coffee Roasters — Maya T., Founder/CEO — 2026-02-11

1. In one paragraph: what does this business do, and for whom?
We roast specialty coffee and get it to two kinds of people: home drinkers who want a great cup delivered on a schedule without thinking about it, and café owners who want a roaster that treats their account like it matters, not like a line item. We're small enough that I still taste every new lot myself.

2. Who are your 2–3 real customer segments? Which one pays the bills?
Subscribers, wholesale café accounts, and a small events/retail bucket. Honestly, subscriptions pay the bills — it's 410 people who don't think twice, the revenue lands every cycle, and it's the healthiest part of the business. Wholesale is exciting and it's where the growth story is, but it's messier money.

3. Why do customers pick you over the alternative?
For subscribers, it's the roast-to-order freshness and the fact that we actually pick up the phone. For cafés, it's that we'll work with them directly — small-batch attention, not a account number at a regional roaster.

4. How does money actually come in? (streams, rough % each)
Subscriptions are about 38%, wholesale is around 45%, and the rest — call it 17% — is events, pop-ups, and one-off retail bags.

5. What 3 resources/capabilities could you not operate without?
Our roaster and the production know-how behind it, our sourcing relationships with a handful of importers, and honestly, me — I still approve every wholesale sample and every unusual spend.
