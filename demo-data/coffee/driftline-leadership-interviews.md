LEADERSHIP INTERVIEW — Driftline Coffee Roasters — Maya T., Founder/CEO — 2026-02-11

1. In one paragraph: what does this business do, and for whom?
We roast specialty coffee and get it to two kinds of people: home drinkers who want a great cup delivered on a schedule without thinking about it, and café owners who want a roaster that treats their account like it matters, not like a line item. We're small enough that I still taste every new lot myself.

2. Who are your 2–3 real customer segments? Which one pays the bills?
Subscribers, wholesale café accounts, and a small events/retail bucket. Honestly, subscriptions pay the bills — it's 410 people who don't think twice, the revenue lands every cycle, and it's the healthiest part of the business. Wholesale is exciting and it's where the growth story is, but it's messier money.

3. Why do customers pick you over the alternative?
For subscribers, it's the roast-to-order freshness and the fact that we actually pick up the phone. For cafés, it's that we'll work with them directly — small-batch attention, not a account number at a regional roaster.

4. How does money actually come in? (streams, rough % each)
Subscriptions are about 38%, wholesale is around 45%, and the rest — call it 17% — is events, pop-ups, and one-off retail bags.

5. What 3 resources/capabilities could you not operate without?
Our roaster and the production know-how behind it, our sourcing relationships with a handful of importers, and honestly, me — I still approve every wholesale sample and every unusual spend.

6. Who are your critical partners/suppliers?
Two green coffee importers we've worked with since year one, our roaster equipment servicer, and Shopify for basically everything customer-facing.

7. What is the single biggest thing limiting growth right now?
Brand awareness, if I'm honest. We could support more subscribers and more wholesale accounts if more people knew we existed. We haven't done real marketing — Rae's doing what she can, but it's one person.

8. What are the 12-month goals, in priority order?
Grow the subscriber base past 500, get wholesale to 40 accounts, and finally get a real marketing budget and maybe a part-time hire for it.

9. What is broken internally that everyone knows and nobody fixes?
Our shipping software throws errors during high-volume weeks and nobody's had time to sit down with Shopify support and actually fix it. It's annoying but we work around it.

10. If a competitor wanted to kill you, what would they attack?
Our price. We're small-batch, which costs more per pound than a regional player, and if someone came in undercutting on wholesale pricing with "good enough" coffee, some cafés might bite.

LEADERSHIP INTERVIEW — Driftline Coffee Roasters — Priya S., Ops & Fulfillment — 2026-02-13

1. In one paragraph: what does this business do, and for whom?
We roast coffee and get it out the door — that's the job I actually own, packing and shipping for subscribers and casing up wholesale orders for cafés. Different customers, same warehouse floor, and lately the floor is the whole story.

2. Who are your 2–3 real customer segments? Which one pays the bills?
Subscribers and wholesale cafés, plus events which barely counts as its own thing. Wholesale pays the bills — it's the bigger revenue line and it's the one growing, even though it's also the one that eats the most of my week. Subscriptions are steady, but wholesale is where the actual dollars are stacking up if we can keep the accounts.

3. Why do customers pick you over the alternative?
Freshness and responsiveness, when we're responsive. Cafés specifically tell us they picked us because we're not a call center — until they're waiting nine days for a quote and start wondering if that was the right call.

4. How does money actually come in? (streams, rough % each)
Roughly what Maya would say — subs high 30s, wholesale mid-40s, events and retail making up the rest. I don't watch the percentages as closely as the case counts I have to pack.

5. What 3 resources/capabilities could you not operate without?
The roaster, packing capacity — we're basically maxed at two packers during wholesale reorder weeks — and honestly a working sample-and-quote process, which we don't have yet.

6. Who are your critical partners/suppliers?
Our shipping carrier, our green coffee importers, and our packaging supplier — we had a scare in Q4 last year when a case-box order was delayed and we almost couldn't fulfill wholesale on time.

7. What is the single biggest thing limiting growth right now?
Our own fulfillment capacity, full stop. We can't turn samples into quotes fast enough, and once an account says yes, packing them into the regular production and shipping schedule is already tight. It's not a demand problem. It's a throughput problem.

8. What are the 12-month goals, in priority order?
Get sample-to-quote under two days instead of nine, put a real owner on wholesale so it's not all routing through Maya's inbox, and then talk about hitting 40 accounts — in that order, because 40 accounts on this process would break us.

9. What is broken internally that everyone knows and nobody fixes?
Wholesale doesn't have an owner. Leads sit until Maya has a free hour to roast a sample herself, quotes go out as one-off emails with pricing she half-remembers from the spreadsheet, and existing accounts reorder by texting her directly at whatever hour. Everyone knows it, nobody's fixed it because Maya's the only one who's ever done it.

10. If a competitor wanted to kill you, what would they attack?
Our reliability on wholesale delivery windows. If a bigger roaster showed up promising a two-day turnaround on quotes and guaranteed reorder dates, some of our accounts would at least take the meeting.
