BIC BENCHMARK — 37signals (public data only)

Real company. Basecamp (project management) and HEY (email) are the flagship products; also ONCE/Campfire/Writebook/Fizzy (self-hosted, "buy once" line). Founded 1999 as a web design studio, pivoted to software, rebranded Basecamp Inc. in 2014, returned to the 37signals name in 2022 as the product line expanded again. Bootstrapped, no outside investors of record beyond an early, pre-independence Jeff Bezos investment; fully remote team. This file contains only claims traceable to a source below, or items explicitly marked assumed/unverified. No invented facts, quotes, or internal details.

---

## 1. Customer Segments
- Small and mid-sized teams and businesses running projects/client work (Basecamp); individuals and, more recently, companies wanting a work email on their own domain (HEY for Work). — Confidence: high. Source: https://basecamp.com , https://www.hey.com/pricing/
- Self-hosters / developers who want to run software on their own servers rather than a SaaS subscription (ONCE line: Campfire, Writebook, Fizzy). — Confidence: high. Source: https://world.hey.com/dhh/once-again-3e99f755

**Delta questions for a bootstrapped vertical SaaS:** Quartermast serves three narrow verticals (plumbing, electrical, garage doors) by design. 37signals deliberately sells horizontal, general-purpose tools to any small team. Is Quartermast's vertical narrowness a moat worth defending, or a ceiling worth breaking through as 37signals-style horizontal players did? At what team size does a horizontal tool start beating a vertical one on total cost of ownership?

## 2. Value Propositions
- Basecamp: one flat-fee, unlimited-user plan instead of climbing per-seat pricing as a team grows — explicitly positioned against per-user billing models. — Confidence: high. Source: https://www.usecarly.com/blog/basecamp-pricing/ (pricing structure corroborated by multiple pricing aggregators; primary source: https://basecamp.com/pricing)
- HEY: an opinionated, screener-first email client that blocks trackers and reorganizes how email is triaged, sold as a stance against "traditional" email as much as a product. — Confidence: high. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/16/basecamp-launches-hey-a-hosted-email-service-for-neat-freaks
- Company-wide stance: calm, sustainable software built by a small team, sold at a fair price, without chasing hypergrowth. — Confidence: high (repeated consistently across founder-authored books and interviews). Source: https://practicalfounders.com/podcast/jason-fried-podcast-20-years-bootstrapping-basecamp-37signals/

**Delta questions:** Quartermast's value proposition today is "cheaper and simpler than the enterprise player." 37signals' flat-fee model punishes vendors who price per seat — could Quartermast's per-seat pricing become a competitive liability against a future flat-fee entrant the way Basecamp positions against per-seat tools? Does Quartermast have anything as opinionated as HEY's screener, or is "simple" currently just "fewer features"?

- QuickBooks-style third-party integrations are not a headline 37signals partnership in the sourced material — their model leans toward owning the full stack (own email client, own project tool, own self-hosting layer) rather than integrating deeply with external financial software. — Confidence: medium (inferred from absence of such partnerships in available sources; not a confirmed negative).

**Delta questions:** Quartermast just lost a deal specifically over QuickBooks integration depth. 37signals appears to minimize dependency on third-party financial-software integrations by owning more of the stack. Is deep QuickBooks integration a permanent tax Quartermast has to pay because of its vertical, or could it reduce that dependency the way 37signals seems to avoid it?

## 9. Cost Structure
- Small, fully remote team keeps fixed headcount costs down relative to output — repeatedly cited by the founders as a deliberate choice, not a constraint. — Confidence: high (consistent founder messaging). Source: https://37signals.com/jobs , https://practicalfounders.com/podcast/jason-fried-podcast-20-years-bootstrapping-basecamp-37signals/
- No paid-marketing-heavy growth spend; growth funded from profits, not fundraising. — Confidence: high. Source: https://www.chicagobusiness.com/article/20120313/BLOGS06/120319945/jason-fried-of-37signals-says-making-money-requires-practice

**Delta questions:** Quartermast's cost structure leans on Dana's sales time and a small CS team stretched across undefined onboarding work. 37signals keeps costs down via a deliberately small, senior, remote team. Is Quartermast's current headcount mix (5 engineers, 1 designer, 2 CS, no dedicated onboarding role) proportioned the way a company at 37signals' philosophy would run it at 15 people, or is it already over-indexed on engineering relative to the customer-facing gap it has?
